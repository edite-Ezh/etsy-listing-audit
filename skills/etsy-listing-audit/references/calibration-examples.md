# Calibration Examples

These examples calibrate judgment. They are patterns, not mandatory wording.

## 1. Hero shows a pair; offer contains one item

**Input:** Lifestyle hero shows two café-curtain panels. Variations say `Width for ONE panel`; description says to choose quantity two for a pair.

**Conclusion:** Major conversion and expectation conflict. Add `PRICE IS FOR 1 PANEL` to the hero or create a two-panel offer matching the image.

**Why:** The visual promise is processed before the description.

## 2. Short product with full-length variants

**Input:** Title and hero promise a short café curtain, but length options extend to floor-length.

**Conclusion:** The current café intent can stay, but full-length curtains deserve a separate hero and listing position.

**Why:** Made-to-order capability supports both products, while one hero cannot prove both outcomes.

## 3. Occasion keyword without product proof

**Input:** Socks are tagged `Christmas gift`, `bed socks` and `therapeutic socks`, but the photos and description only prove warm wool socks.

**Conclusion:** Do not create duplicate listings from the words alone. Retain or split the intent only after adding a Christmas gift presentation, bedtime context, or a substantiated comfort benefit.

**Why:** A keyword is not a distinct offer.

## 4. Materials field used as SEO storage

**Input:** Materials list says `linen, natural textile, breathable material, durable fabric, sustainable decor`.

**Conclusion:** Replace benefit phrases with the exact composition, weave/treatment and certification when verified.

**Why:** Materials should reduce uncertainty, not duplicate keywords.

## 5. New-looking Etsy date with established demand

**Input:** Footer says `Listed on` today, while EverBee reports a 19-month age and the listing has many historical reviews.

**Conclusion:** Treat the footer date as a renewal/relisting event, not proof that the listing is new.

**Why:** Multiple historical signals contradict a new-listing interpretation.

## 6. Fixed shipping cost across a wide price range

**Input:** Shipping is €6; smallest variant costs €20.52 and largest costs €162.14.

**Conclusion:** Shipping is 29.2% of the entry product price but only 3.7% of the largest price. Report both instead of calling shipping simply cheap or expensive.

**Why:** The buyer's perception changes by selected variant.

## 7. Multiple constructions hidden in personalization

**Input:** Title says rod pocket, while the buyer can type rod pocket, tab top, tie top or heading tape in personalization.

**Conclusion:** The offer is flexible but error-prone. Show actual photos of every construction and consider separate construction-specific hero listings.

**Why:** A generic illustration does not prove the delivered finish.

## 8. High favorites with low conversion

**Input:** A visually attractive listing has thousands of favorites but an estimated conversion below 0.5%.

**Conclusion:** Demand for the look exists, but purchase friction is likely. Investigate quantity, customization, pricing and delivery clarity before broadening SEO.

**Why:** More traffic will not repair a confusing offer.
